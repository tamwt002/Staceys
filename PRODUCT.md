# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Curious locals — Newtown regulars and inner-west passers-by who want to know if Tino's is worth dropping into. They arrive via word of mouth, a mention in Broadsheet, or walking past the shopfront. They care about vibe, hours, and whether the place is for them — tasting note depth is secondary. The site's job is to make the shop feel worth the detour, not to educate.

## Product Purpose

Online presence for a small, single-location specialty tea shop at 14 King Street, Newtown NSW. The site surfaces the menu, location, hours, and enough personality to convert a curious local into a first visit. Success looks like someone reading the about copy, skimming the menu, and deciding to walk in.

## Brand Personality

Quietly luxurious. The shop is genuinely excellent but doesn't announce it — excellence is implied by specificity (the estate names, the cultivar notes, the exact steep times), not by language like "premium" or "artisan". The experience is unhurried and knowledgeable without being intimidating.

Three words: considered, specific, unhurried.

## Anti-references

- **T2 / David Jones tea aisle**: mass-market, gift-wrapped wellness, "treat yourself" register. Too cheerful, too product-volume-focused.
- **Gong Cha / bubble tea chains**: casual, quick-service, trend-driven. Wrong price point and mood entirely.
- **Generic "warm artisan" web template**: cream background, script font, three-column feature grid, stock photography of tea leaves. This is the default AI output and is explicitly what this site was built to avoid.

## Design Principles

1. **Show expertise, don't name it.** The menu's precision — origin, estate, tasting notes, steep parameters — does the signalling. Never use the words "premium", "artisan", "curated", or "craft".
2. **The shop comes first.** Every design choice should make the physical space feel worth visiting. Hours, address, and the actual menu are the highest-value elements on the page.
3. **Restraint is the luxury signal.** No drop shadows, no rounded cards, no gradient text. Negative space and typographic control do what decoration would cheapen.
4. **Specificity over warmth.** "Halfway between the station and the pub that plays jazz on Sundays" lands harder than "Welcome to Tino's Tea." Concrete detail beats brand-speak.
5. **One deliberate moment.** If something moves or draws attention, it should be singular and purposeful — not a system of micro-interactions.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Keyboard navigation fully supported with visible focus states (amber outline). `prefers-reduced-motion` respected — the one load animation (rule draw) falls back to instant display. No known specific user needs beyond standard web accessibility.
